# Prompt library

## 1. Small-study design brief

Create a concise design brief from the supplied facts. Include: research question, rationale, objectives, study design, setting, eligibility, intervention/exposure and comparator, outcomes and timepoints, recruitment/retention plan, safety monitoring, analysis outline, feasibility criteria, ethics/registration placeholders, limitations, and decisions still needed. Do not invent values.

## 2. Pilot or feasibility protocol synopsis

Draft a protocol synopsis suited to a small clinical study. Use sections for purpose, objectives, design, participants, procedures, outcome definitions, visit schedule, data handling, safety reporting, analysis, oversight, and dissemination. Explicitly separate feasibility objectives from clinical-effectiveness claims.

## 3. Analysis or sample-size rationale

Build an analysis-plan outline or a sample-size rationale. State the primary decision question, analysis population, endpoint type, summary measures, missing-data approach, safety summaries, and planned sensitivity/exploratory analyses. When values are incomplete, list the parameters required; do not calculate a number.

## 4. Conference abstract

Draft a structured abstract using the meeting's required headings and length. Preserve exact data and denominators. Cover objective, design/setting, methods, results, safety or failures where relevant, limitations, and conclusion calibrated to the evidence. Finish with a disclosure/verification checklist.

## 5. Poster, talk, or proceedings manuscript

Transform supplied material into an audience-appropriate outline. For posters: title, take-home message, methods diagram, results panels, limitations, disclosures, and next step. For talks: opening problem, methods, evidence, limitations, conclusion, and discussion slide. For proceedings: follow the organizer's structure and preserve evidence provenance.

## 6. Submission readiness review

Create a checklist for the specified meeting: formatting, word/figure limits, authorship, blinding, ethics, consent, trial registration, disclosures, funding, data availability, and deadline. Mark every time-sensitive or meeting-specific item `[VERIFY WITH ORGANIZER]` unless officially confirmed.

## 7. Device prototype evaluation plan

Draft an evaluation plan that begins with intended use and the correct evidence level. Specify prototype version, users, use environment, tasks, hazards/risk controls, evaluation design, acceptance criteria, metrics, test records, deviations, adverse events, and limitations. Avoid regulatory or marketing conclusions.

## 8. Prototype evidence report

Write a factual report for bench, simulated-use, usability, or feasibility evidence. Include prototype version, methods, sample/tests, endpoints, results, failures/use errors/adverse events, deviations, limitations, and next evidence needed. Label unsupported fields `[NEEDS INPUT]`.

## 9. Research-quality review

Review a supplied draft against the quality gates. Return a prioritized table: issue, why it matters, evidence needed, suggested revision, and reviewer owner. Separate must-fix integrity issues from optional clarity improvements.
